## Search protocol
We started the search process by exploring related work we were already familiar with based on our previous work [1]. Beyond our own knowledge of related research and exploratory searches on Google Scholar, we also applied a systematic approach to improve our coverage of related papers. Specifically, we reviewed the last three to four editions (2023 and onwards) of various leading conferences and journals in software engineering. The Excel file “search_venues.xlsx” contains an overview of those venues and the tracks we reviewed.

To determine whether a paper is relevant, we first reviewed the titles to identify specific keywords. We focused on the following topics: measuring the energy consumption of LLMs, classifying issues, and classifying app reviews using LLMs. In addition, we searched for papers that relate to the topic of batching input within prompts. The specific keywords considered are also listed in the Excel file “search_venues.xlsx”. When a paper matched a combination of keywords, we examined the abstract more closely to determine its relevance. For example, even if the title indicates that the topic relates to issues and large language models, it still needs to be confirmed whether the topic involves classification, or whether the keyword “issue” in a title refer to issues from an issue-tracking system. After reviewing the abstract, we took a closer look at the paper's content to assess its relevance.

[1] Ashley T Van Can, Fatma Başak Aydemir, and Fabiano Dalpiaz. 2025. One size does not fit all: On the role of batch size in classifying requirements with LLMs. In International Requirements Engineering Conference Workshops. IEEE Computer Society, IEEE, 30–39. doi:10.1109/REW66121.2025.00009
